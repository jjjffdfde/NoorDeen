## 1. Executive Summary

While the foundational narrative of the life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is established through universal consensus (*Ijma'*) and corroborated by the Holy Quran and canonical Sahihayn Hadith corpora, certain chronological dates, minor event durations, participant figures, and biographical details exhibit scholarly divergence among classical historians (*Ahl al-Siyar wal-Maghazi*).

In accordance with Phase 2 editorial standards and Phase 4 research integrity directives, this document identifies, catalogs, and transparently annotates all items of historical divergence present in the 25-chapter Seerah corpus. Each item is classified under `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` with documented scholarly positions, classical sources, and editorial rationale.

## 2. Catalog of Scholarly Divergences & Review Items

### ITEM-01: Exact Day and Month of the Prophet's Blessed Birth
- **Related Chapter:** Chapter 2 (`#ch2`)
- **Consensus Element:** Born on a **Monday** in the **Year of the Elephant** (approximately 570 CE) into Banu Hashim. Prophet confirmed fasting on Monday because it was his day of birth (Sahih Muslim #1162).
- **Disputed Element:** The exact calendar day of Rabi al-Awwal.
- **Scholarly Positions:**
  1. *12th of Rabi al-Awwal:* The most widely accepted public commemoration date in classical chronicles (Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Hisham, Ibn Sa'd).
  2. *9th of Rabi al-Awwal:* Calculated by modern astronomical historians (Mahmud Pasha al-Falaki, supported by Safiur-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri in *Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum*), arguing that Monday 12th Rabi al-Awwal did not coincide with the solar calendar in 570 CE.
  3. *8th of Rabi al-Awwal:* Preferred by Ibn Hazm, Al-Humaydi, and reported from Ibn Abbas and Jubayr ibn Mut'im.
  4. *2nd or 10th of Rabi al-Awwal:* Recorded in secondary historical compilations (Al-Waqidi).
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Recorded as the classical consensus date (Monday 12th of Rabi al-Awwal) while noting general historical proximity.
- **Review Status:** `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` for prospective scholarly footnote toggle.

### ITEM-02: Age of Sayyidah Khadijah bint Khuwaylid (RA) at Marriage
- **Related Chapter:** Chapter 5 (`#ch5`)
- **Consensus Element:** Muhammad ﷺ was twenty-five years of age; the marriage was solemnized by Abu Talib; their marital union lasted twenty-five years of devoted monogamy until her passing.
- **Disputed Element:** Sayyidah Khadijah's age at the time of marriage.
- **Scholarly Positions:**
  1. *Forty (40) Years Old:* The predominant classical transmission reported by Al-Waqidi and Ibn Sa'd (*At-Tabaqat al-Kubra* 8/17).
  2. *Twenty-Eight (28) Years Old:* Transmitted by Ibn Ishaq (*Kitab al-Maghazi*) through Hakim ibn Hizam (Khadijah's nephew), favored by Ibn Kathir (*Al-Bidayah wan-Nihayah* 2/295), Adh-Dhahabi (*Siyar A'lam an-Nubala*), and modern critical historians based on her bearing six children following the marriage.
  3. *Thirty-Five (35) or Forty-Five (45) Years Old:* Variant secondary reports in classical chronicles.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Mentions the predominant classical transmission (forty) while explicitly citing classical variant sources noting twenty-eight in parentheses.
- **Review Status:** `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` for final editorial consensus preference.

### ITEM-03: Duration of the Cessation of Revelation (Fatrah al-Wahy)
- **Related Chapter:** Chapter 8 (`#ch8`)
- **Consensus Element:** Revelation temporarily ceased following the inaugural revelation in Cave Hira, causing the Prophet intense grief, before resuming with the opening of Surah Al-Muddaththir (Sahih al-Bukhari #3, #4).
- **Disputed Element:** The exact duration of the cessation period.
- **Scholarly Positions:**
  1. *A Few Days to Forty Days:* Strongly argued by Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani (*Fath al-Bari* 1/27) and Al-Bayhaqi, who note that a multi-year gap contradicts the rapid early propagation.
  2. *Two and a Half to Three Years:* Transmitted by Ash-Sha'bi and recorded by Ibn Sa'd; however, modern researchers including Al-Mubarakpuri demonstrate that this report conflates the period of private preaching with the initial cessation of Wahy.
  3. *Six Months:* Calculated by some scholars as bridging Ramadan to Rabi al-Awwal.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Described objectively as an agonizing temporary period ranging from several days to several months, avoiding dogmatic commitment to the rejected three-year cessation narrative.
- **Review Status:** `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` for inclusion of comparative scholarly note.

### ITEM-04: Exact Year and Date of Al-Isra wal-Mi'raj
- **Related Chapter:** Chapter 13 (`#ch13`)
- **Consensus Element:** A bodily, waking night journey from Makkah to Jerusalem aboard Al-Buraq, leading the prophets in prayer, celestial ascension through seven heavens to Sidrat al-Muntaha, and ordination of five daily prayers (Surah Al-Isra 17:1; Surah An-Najm 53:1–18; Sahih al-Bukhari #3887).
- **Disputed Element:** Exact calendar year and month of the event.
- **Scholarly Positions:**
  1. *27th of Rajab, 10th or 11th Year of Prophethood (16 to 18 months before Hijrah):* The most widely observed traditional date across the Muslim world, recorded by Ibn Sa'd and supported by Al-Alusi.
  2. *Month of Rabi al-Awwal (1 year before Hijrah):* Transmitted by Az-Zuhri and Urwah ibn az-Zubayr.
  3. *Month of Ramadan (18 months or 3 years before Hijrah):* Recorded by Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shaybah.
  4. *Month of Shawwal or Dhu al-Hijjah:* Reported by secondary biographical traditions.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Anchored chronologically between the Year of Sorrow (619 CE) and the Pledges of Al-Aqabah (620–621 CE), citing the traditional 27th Rajab tradition while emphasizing theological consensus on the event itself.
- **Review Status:** `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` regarding celebratory calendar date designations.

### ITEM-05: Exact Number of Believers at the Battle of Badr
- **Related Chapter:** Chapter 17 (`#ch17`)
- **Consensus Element:** The Muslim contingent was approximately 310 to 317 men, with two horses and seventy camels, facing one thousand fully armored Qurayshi warriors.
- **Disputed Element:** The precise headcount of the Muslim army.
- **Scholarly Positions:**
  1. *313 Men:* The famous number transmitted in Sahih al-Bukhari (#3957) from Al-Bara' ibn Azib: "We the companions of Muhammad used to say that the number of the fighters of Badr was the same as the number of the companions of Saul (Talut) who crossed the river: 313 men."
  2. *314 Men:* Transmitted in Musnad Ahmad (#378).
  3. *317 Men:* Recorded by Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Hisham (*As-Seerah* 1/677).
  4. *319 Men:* Recorded in variant narrations by At-Tabari.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Expressed as "a mere 313 to 317 men", maintaining full harmony with canonical Hadith while acknowledging minor textual variations.
- **Review Status:** `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` — No further changes recommended as standard practice encompasses this range.

### ITEM-06: Nature and Terms of the Judgement on Banu Qurayzah
- **Related Chapter:** Chapter 19 (`#ch19`)
- **Consensus Element:** Banu Qurayzah breached the Charter of Madinah during the existential siege of the Trench, entering treasonous coordination with the Confederates; their surrender followed a 25-day siege; judgment was rendered by their own former ally Sa'd ibn Mu'adh (RA).
- **Disputed Element:** Application and historical scope of the sentence.
- **Scholarly Positions:**
  1. *Classical Consensus:* Sa'd judged according to biblical law (Deuteronomy 20:10–14) that combatant men be executed and dependents taken captive. The Prophet affirmed: "You have judged among them with the ruling of the Sovereign King (Allah)" (Sahih al-Bukhari #3043).
  2. *Modern Revisionist Critique:* Scholars like W.N. Arafat and Barakat Ahmad argued that the classical figures (600–900 executed) were exaggerated through uncritical transmission from descendants of Qurayzah, suggesting that only active treason conspirators (the leaders and armed combatants, ~40–100 men) were executed.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Preserved the classical Sahihayn narrative of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh's arbitration while contextualizing it strictly within the treasonous breach of treaty during an existential wartime siege.
- **Review Status:** `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW` for prospective scholarly commentary or historiographical note.

## 3. Methodological Principles for Handling Historical Divergences

The following five rules govern future revisions and editorial sign-offs on disputed Seerah matters:

1. **Hierarchy of Evidence:**
   $$\text{Quran} \gg \text{Mutawatir Hadith} \gg \text{Sahihayn} \gg \text{Sunan/Musnad} \gg \text{Early Maghazi (Ibn Hisham/Ibn Sa'd)} \gg \text{Secondary Chronicles}$$
2. **Textual Transparency:** Where classical scholars differed (e.g., Khadijah's age at marriage or exact date of birth), both the predominant view and the primary variant report must be mentioned without dogmatic denunciation.
3. **Defense Against Fabrication:** Any narrative containing unverified miraculous claims unsupported by authentic Isnad (e.g., idols falling down across the world on the night of birth, or talking animals not authenticated in Sahih collections) is excluded from the canonical text.
4. **Contextualization of Wartime Verdicts:** Legal judgments and military encounters must be firmly anchored within their 7th-century geopolitical and defensive legal reality to prevent anachronistic misinterpretation.
5. **No Speculative Dialogue:** Absolutely no speculative emotions, thoughts, or fabricated dialogues are attributed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ or his companions.
