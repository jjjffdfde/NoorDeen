## 2. Standardized Biography Structural Contract

Every companion biography conforms to an eleven-part structural contract ensuring rigorous academic and religious integrity:

1. **Header Metadata:**
   - Stable biographical DOM anchor ID (e.g. `id="abu-bakr"`, `id="talhah"`, `id="khadijah"`).
   - Category badge (`Khulafa ar-Rashidun`, `Promised Paradise`, `Mothers of the Believers & Women Companions`, `Eminent Scholars & Scribes`, `Stalwarts of Sacrifice`).
   - Arabic name with authentic calligraphy and diacritics (e.g., أبو بكر الصديق رضي الله عنه).
   - English title with Kunya / honorific (e.g., *As-Siddiq*, *Al-Faruq*, *Dhun-Nurayn*, *Hawari Rasulillah*).
   - Chronological lifespan and historical dates in both AH (Hijri) and CE (Common Era).
2. **Reading Time Estimate:** Realistic indicator based on standard adult reading speed (e.g., `⏱️ 5 min read`).
3. **Historical Origins & Pre-Islamic Context:** Lineage, clan background, early moral reputation, and circumstances prior to the light of revelation.
4. **Embracing Islam & Early Fortitude:** The turning point of conversion, sacrifices endured, steadfastness in the face of Makkan persecution, or pioneering participation in the early secret assemblies of Dar al-Arqam.
5. **Major Historical Services & Pivotal Milestones:** Comprehensive narration of decisive contributions during the Prophetic mission (e.g., Hijrah, Badr, Uhud, Khandaq, Hudaybiyyah, Khaybar, Tabuk, or civil pacts).
6. **Leadership, Governance & Intellectual Legacy:** Documented roles in post-Prophetic leadership, caliphate administration, Quranic compilation, Hadith transmission, judicial verdicts, military command, or societal philanthropy.
7. **Passing, Demise & Character Memorial:** Sourced accounts of their final hours, martyrdom or peaceful passing, place of resting, and final testamentary words.
8. **Quranic & Hadith Cross-Reference Box:**
   - Explicit Arabic Quranic verses or authentic Hadith honoring their virtues.
   - Verified English translation with exact Surah and Ayah / Hadith collection number.
   - Theological commentary explaining the context of the divine praise.
9. **Key Historical Achievements Box:** Structured bulleted inventory highlighting 4 to 6 distinctive contributions.
10. **Primary Classical Citations Footer:** Detailed academic citations referencing *Al-Isabah fi Tamyiz as-Sahabah* of Ibn Hajar, *Usd al-Ghabah* of Ibn al-Athir, *At-Tabaqat al-Kubra* of Ibn Sa'd, *Siyar A'lam an-Nubala* of Adh-Dhahabi, and canonical *Sahih al-Bukhari* / *Sahih Muslim*.
11. **Theological Verification Badge:** Explicit consensus rating (`✓ Verified: Muttafaqun 'Alayh` or `✓ Verified: Classical Consensus`).

## 3. Thematic Categories & Inclusion Justification

The 30 biographies are distributed across five historically authentic cohorts:

### Cohort 1: The Four Rightly Guided Caliphs (Al-Khulafa ar-Rashidun) [4 Biographies]
- **Subjects:** Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (`abu-bakr`), Umar ibn al-Khattab (`umar`), Uthman ibn Affan (`uthman`), Ali ibn Abi Talib (`ali`).
- **Justification:** The supreme historical pillars who steered the Muslim Ummah following the Prophet's demise, instituted public welfare treasuries, codified the lunar calendar, unified the Mushaf, and embodied egalitarian Islamic governance.

### Cohort 2: The Foremost Muhajirun & Promised Paradise (Al-Asharah al-Mubashsharah) [6 Biographies]
- **Subjects:** Talhah ibn Ubaydullah (`talhah`), Az-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam (`zubayr`), Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf (`abdur-rahman`), Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (`sad-ibn-abi-waqqas`), Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah (`abu-ubaydah`), Sa'id ibn Zayd (`said-ibn-zayd`).
- **Justification:** Completes the canonical Ten Promised Paradise (*Al-Asharah al-Mubashsharun bil-Jannah*), resolving the baseline gap where six of these foremost companions were omitted.

### Cohort 3: Mothers of the Believers & Prominent Women Companions [10 Biographies]
- **Subjects:** Khadijah bint Khuwaylid (`khadijah`), Aisha bint Abi Bakr (`aisha`), Fatimah az-Zahra (`fatima`), Umm Salamah Hind (`umm-salamah`), Asma bint Abi Bakr (`asma`), Hafsah bint Umar (`hafsah`), Umm Sulaym bint Milhan (`umm-sulaym`), Nusaybah bint Ka'b (`nusaybah`), Sumayyah bint Khayyat (`sumayyah`), Al-Shifa bint Abdullah (`shifa`).
- **Justification:** Restores comprehensive gender balance in early Islamic history, documenting women as intellectual jurists, political advisors, educators, medical caretakers, battlefield defenders, and market supervisors.

### Cohort 4: Eminent Scholars, Scribes & Jurists [6 Biographies]
- **Subjects:** Abdullah ibn Abbas (`ibn-abbas`), Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (`ibn-masud`), Mu'adh ibn Jabal (`muadh-ibn-jabal`), Zayd ibn Thabit (`zayd-ibn-thabit`), Abu Hurayrah (`abu-hurayrah`), Abu Dharr al-Ghifari (`abu-dharr`).
- **Justification:** Highlights the foundational intellectual architects of Islamic civilization: the premier exegete of the Quran (Ibn Abbas), the pioneer of Kufan jurisprudence (Ibn Mas'ud), the master of Halal and Haram (Mu'adh), the chief scribe of the Uthmanic Mushaf (Zayd), the most prolific Hadith narrator (Abu Hurayrah), and the prophetic voice of social equity (Abu Dharr).

### Cohort 5: Stalwarts of Sacrifice, Leadership & Service [4 Biographies]
- **Subjects:** Bilal ibn Rabah (`bilal`), Salman al-Farsi (`salman`), Khalid ibn al-Walid (`khalid`), Mus'ab ibn Umayr (`musab`).
- **Justification:** Exemplars of steadfast moral courage: the voice of liberation (Bilal), the Persian seeker of truth and siege tactician (Salman), the undefeated military strategist (Khalid), and the gentle aristocratic ambassador to Madinah (Mus'ab).
