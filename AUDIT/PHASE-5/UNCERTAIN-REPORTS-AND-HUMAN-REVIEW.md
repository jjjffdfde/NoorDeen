## 2. Catalog of Scholarly Variances & Human Review Items

### Item 1: Theological Status of Luqman al-Hakim (Prophet vs. Sage)
- **Subject:** `id="luqman"` (Luqman the Wise)
- **Theological Question:** Was Luqman an ordained prophet (Nabi) or an extraordinarily righteous saint (Wali / Hakim)?
- **Classical Scholarly Evidence:**
  - *Majority Consensus:* Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, and Ibn Kathir (*Tafsir* 6/330) affirm that Luqman was an Ethiopian/Nubian sage and saint gifted with profound wisdom, not a commissioned prophet.
  - *Minority View:* A report attributed to Ikrimah and Ash-Sha'bi suggested prophetic status based on the honorific treatment in Surah 31.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Classified under `exemplary-figures` as an Exemplary Sage (*Hakim*). The text explicitly presents the mainstream consensus: *"The majority of classical scholars... hold that Luqman was not an ordained prophet, but an extraordinarily righteous saint."*
- **Status:** `PASS — SCHOLARLY NUANCE DOCUMENTED`

### Item 2: Theological Status and Historical Identity of Dhul-Qarnayn
- **Subject:** `id="dhul-qarnayn"` (The Possessor of Two Horns)
- **Theological & Historical Questions:** 
  1. Was Dhul-Qarnayn a prophet, an angel, or a righteous monarch?
  2. Is Dhul-Qarnayn identical to Alexander the Great (Alexander of Macedon)?
- **Classical Scholarly Evidence:**
  - *Prophetic Hadith:* The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ stated: *"I do not know whether Tubba' was cursed or not, and I do not know whether Dhul-Qarnayn was a prophet or not"* (*Sunan Abi Dawud* #4674, authenticated by Al-Albani).
  - *Ibn Kathir & Ibn Taymiyyah:* Vigorously refuted identifying Dhul-Qarnayn with Alexander of Macedon. Alexander was a Greek pagan polytheist tutored by Aristotle (reigning c. 330 BCE), whereas Dhul-Qarnayn was an ancient monotheistic ruler living during an earlier era (traditionally contemporary with Ibrahim).
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Classified as a righteous monotheistic monarch (*Malik Salih*). The text unequivocally repudiates the conflation with Alexander the Great and notes the hadith regarding his prophetic vs. royal status.
- **Status:** `PASS — SCHOLARLY NUANCE DOCUMENTED`

### Item 3: Theological Status of Maryam bint Imran (Prophetess vs. Siddiqah)
- **Subject:** `id="maryam"` (Maryam bint Imran)
- **Theological Question:** Did Maryam possess prophethood (*Nubuwwat an-Nisa'*), or was she the paramount *Siddiqah* (Truthful Saint)?
- **Classical Scholarly Evidence:**
  - *Majority Sunnite Consensus:* Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari, Al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibn Kathir (*Tafsir* 2/45), and Ibn Taymiyyah assert that prophethood was restricted to men based on Surah Yusuf: *"And We sent not before you except men whom We inspired"* (12:109) and Surah An-Nahl (16:43). Her title in revelation is *Al-Siddiqah* (Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:75).
  - *Minority View:* Ibn Hazm (*Al-Fasl fil-Milal*) and Al-Qurtubi (*Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Quran*) argued for female prophethood based on the angelic visitation and direct divine address (Surah Ali 'Imran 3:42).
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Classified under `exemplary-figures` as *Al-Siddiqah* (The Truthful Saint), honoring her supreme station among women while upholding the majority Sunnite creed that prophethood was assigned to men.
- **Status:** `PASS — SCHOLARLY NUANCE DOCUMENTED`

### Item 4: Identity and Prophethood of Dhul-Kifl
- **Subject:** `id="dhul-kifl"`
- **Historical & Exegetical Question:** Who was Dhul-Kifl, and was he the biblical Ezekiel (*Hizqil*), Joshua (*Yusha*), or a pious guarantor (*Kafil*) under Al-Yasa'?
- **Classical Scholarly Evidence:**
  - *Tafsir Ibn Kathir* (5/369): Because Dhul-Kifl is mentioned alongside Ismail and Idris in Surah Al-Anbiya (21:85) and Surah Sad (38:48), the apparent context (*Zahir al-Siyāq*) indicates prophethood. Many historians identify him with Ezekiel.
  - *Tafsir at-Tabari* (18/510): Mujahid narrated that he was an upright man of Banu Israel who pledged to fast every day, pray every night, and judge without anger, fulfilling the covenant (*Kifl*).
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** The monograph explicitly presents both interpretations with scholarly restraint: *"Scholarly opinion among classical exegetes is divided regarding his exact historical identity and whether he was an ordained prophet or an extraordinarily righteous saint..."*
- **Status:** `PASS — SCHOLARLY NUANCE DOCUMENTED`

### Item 5: Identity of the Sacrificial Son (Ad-Dhabih: Ismail vs. Ishaq)
- **Subject:** `id="ismail"` and `id="ishaq"`
- **Exegetical Dispute:** Which son of Ibrahim was commanded to be sacrificed?
- **Classical Scholarly Evidence:**
  - *Sunnite Consensus:* Ibn Kathir (*Tafsir* 7/25–35), Ibn al-Qayyim (*Zad al-Ma'ad* 1/71), and the vast majority of commentators confirm it was **Ismail**. The proof is contextual: in Surah As-Saffat (37:101–112), Allah narrates the sacrifice of the firstborn son, accepts Ibrahim's obedience, and *subsequently* announces: *"And We gave him the good tidings of Isaac, a prophet among the righteous"* (37:112).
  - *Isra'iliyyat Influence:* Early reports in At-Tabari cite certain narrations suggesting Isaac, which scholars demonstrated were influenced by Jewish transmissions seeking to claim the blessing for Isaac's descendants.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** The portal firmly presents Ismail as *Ad-Dhabih* (The Sacrificed One) in accordance with the Quranic textual sequence and authoritative Hadith consensus, while clarifying the historical context.
- **Status:** `PASS — CONSENSUS AFFIRMED`

### Item 7: Ethical Restraint on Companions' Political Disputes (*Kaff al-Lisan*)
- **Subject:** Sahaba Monograph Cohorts (Ali, Talhah, Az-Zubayr, Aisha, Mu'awiyah, etc.)
- **Theological Principle:** The orthodox Sunnite doctrine formulated by Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari, and Imam an-Nawawi mandates *Kaff al-Lisan* (restraint of speech) regarding the civil conflicts (Fitnah) among the Companions. They are held to be sincere *Mujtahidun* (exercising personal juridical judgment), where the correct party receives two rewards and the mistaken party receives one reward and divine pardon.
- **Portal Editorial Treatment:** Monograph biographies focus on their immense virtues, pre-eminence in early Islam, and legal/spiritual contributions, strictly eschewing sectarian polemics, defamatory language, or unverified dramatizations.
- **Status:** `PASS — SUNNITE ADAB STRICTLY MAINTAINED`
