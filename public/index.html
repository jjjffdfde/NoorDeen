قاموس المصطلحات الإسلامية • Encyclopedic Terminology Hub

📚 Interactive Islamic Glossary & Terminology (135 Essential Terms)

Source-verified definitions, exact Arabic vocalizations, phonetic transliterations, and classical citations across theology, jurisprudence, and character

Tawheed

Tawḥīd
تَوْحِيد

The foundational doctrine of the absolute, indivisible oneness and uniqueness of Allah. Comprises three classical dimensions: Tawheed ar-Rububiyyah (Oneness in Lordship and creation), Tawheed al-Uluhiyyah (Oneness in exclusive worship), and Tawheed al-Asma wa-s-Sifat (Oneness in His pristine Names and Attributes).

Reference: Surah Al-Ikhlas 112:1-4; Kitab at-Tawhid by Imam Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab

Shirk

Shirk
شِرْك

The ultimate unforgivable theological transgression of associating partners, equals, or intermediaries with Allah in His divine essence, lordship, or exclusive worship. Categorized into Major Shirk (Shirk Akbar) which nullifies faith, and Minor Shirk (Shirk Asghar) such as showing off (Riya').

Reference: Surah An-Nisa 4:48, 116; Sahih al-Bukhari #4777

Iman

Īmān
إِيمَان

Faith consisting of firm conviction of the heart (I'tiqad), verbal affirmation upon the tongue (Qawl), and practical demonstration through bodily limbs (Amal). Increases with righteous obedience and decreases with disobedience.

Reference: Surah Al-Hujurat 49:15; Sahih al-Bukhari #9

Ihsan

Iḥsān
إِحْسَان

Spiritual perfection and mindfulness defined by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as: 'To worship Allah as though you see Him, and if you do not see Him, truly He sees you.' Represents the apex of spiritual realization.

Reference: Sahih Muslim #8 (Hadith Jibril); Sahih al-Bukhari #50

Nifaq

Nifāq
نِفَاق

Hypocrisy. Divided into doctrinal hypocrisy (Nifaq I'tiqadi), which conceals inner disbelief while feigning Islam, and practical hypocrisy (Nifaq 'Amali), involving traits such as lying when speaking, breaking promises, and betraying trusts.

Reference: Surah An-Nisa 4:145; Sahih al-Bukhari #33

Qadar

Qadar
قَدَر

The sixth pillar of faith: divine predestination and measurement. Comprises four levels: divine eternal knowledge ('Ilm), divine recording in the Preserved Tablet (Kitabah), divine sovereign will (Mashi'ah), and divine creation of all events (Khalq).

Reference: Surah Al-Qamar 54:49; Sahih Muslim #8

Tawakkul

Tawakkul
تَوَكُّل

True reliant trust in Allah while actively pursuing legitimate physical means (Asbab). Exemplified by the Prophetic adage: 'Tie your camel, and then place your trust in Allah.'

Reference: Surah Ali 'Imran 3:159; Jami` at-Tirmidhi #2517

Ghayb

Ghayb
غَيْب

The unseen realm inaccessible to physical human senses, known comprehensively only to Allah. Encompasses the angels, the Preserved Tablet, the Day of Judgment, Paradise, Hellfire, and the nature of the soul.

Reference: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:3; Surah Al-An'am 6:59

Barzakh

Barzakh
بَرْزَخ

The intermediate realm and barrier between physical earthly demise and the universal bodily Resurrection on the Day of Judgment, where souls experience a foretaste of their ultimate eternal destination.

Reference: Surah Al-Mu'minun 23:100; Sahih al-Bukhari #1374

Mizan

Mīzān
مِيزَان

The cosmic literal Scale established on the Day of Resurrection upon which human intentions, records of deeds, and the doers themselves will be weighed with absolute divine justice.

Reference: Surah Al-Anbiya 21:47; Sahih al-Bukhari #6405

Sirat

Ṣirāṭ
صِرَاط

The razor-sharp, narrow bridge suspended over the abyss of Hellfire which every soul must cross on the Day of Resurrection. Believers traverse it with the speed of lightning, wind, or running horses in proportion to their righteous deeds.

Reference: Surah Maryam 19:71-72; Sahih Muslim #195

Hawd

Ḥawḍ
حَوْض

The celestial drinking fountain granted to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ on the Day of Judgment. Its water is whiter than milk, cooler than snow, and sweeter than honey; whoever drinks from it will never thirst again.

Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari #6579; Sahih Muslim #2292

Shafa'ah

Shafā'ah
شَفَاعَة

Intercession granted by divine permission on the Day of Judgment. The greatest is Al-Maqam al-Mahmud (the Praised Station) reserved for Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to inaugurate universal divine reckoning.

Reference: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:255; Sahih al-Bukhari #4718

Rububiyyah

Rubūbiyyah
رُبُوبِيَّة

The belief that Allah alone is the Lord, Creator, Sustainer, Sovereign, and Controller of all existence without partner.

Reference: Surah Al-Fatihah 1:2; Surah Al-A'raf 7:54

Uluhiyyah

Ulūhiyyah
أُلُوهِيَّة

The doctrine that Allah alone is worthy of all worship, prayer, sacrifice, vows, and devotional hope and fear.

Reference: Surah An-Nahl 16:36; Surah Al-Anbiya 21:25

Asma wa Sifat

Asmā' wa-ṣ-Ṣifāt
أَسْمَاء وَصِفَات

Affirming all the Names and Attributes that Allah and His Messenger ﷺ have affirmed for Him, without distortion (Tahreef), denial (Ta'teel), asking how (Takyif), or likening to creation (Tamthil).

Reference: Surah Ash-Shura 42:11 ('There is nothing like unto Him')

Fitrah

Fiṭrah
فِطْرَة

The primordial innate natural disposition imprinted upon every human soul at creation, innately recognizing the existence of the One Creator.

Reference: Surah Ar-Rum 30:30; Sahih al-Bukhari #1358

Wahy

Waḥy
وَحْي

Divine inspiration and revelation communicated from Allah to His chosen prophets through Angel Jibril, direct divine speech, or truthful dreams.

Reference: Surah Ash-Shura 42:51; Sahih al-Bukhari #1-3

Khatam an-Nabiyyin

Khātam an-Nabiyyīn
خَاتَم النَّبِيِّين

The Seal and Finality of the Prophets: the decisive Islamic dogma establishing Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as the ultimate and final messenger of Allah, after whom no prophet will ever appear.

Reference: Surah Al-Ahzab 33:40; Sahih Muslim #2286

Yawm al-Qiyamah

Yawm al-Qiyāmah
يَوْم القِيَامَة

The Day of Resurrection: the universal cosmic reckoning when all humanity is raised bodily from their graves to stand before Allah.

Reference: Surah Al-Qiyamah 75:1-40; Sahih al-Bukhari #6526

Ba'th

Ba'th
بَعْث

The universal bodily resurrection of all dead beings from the earth upon the second sounding of the celestial Horn (Soor) by Angel Israfil.

Reference: Surah Ya-Sin 36:51-53; Surah Al-Hajj 22:7

Salah

Ṣalāh
صَلَاة

The ritual prescribed prayer performed five times daily. Constitutes the second pillar of Islam and the primary daily communion between the created servant and the Creator.

Reference: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:43; Sahih al-Bukhari #8

Wudu

Wuḍū'
وُضُوء

Ritual ablution performed with pure water prior to prayer, circumambulation, and handling scripture. Includes washing the face, arms to elbows, wiping the head, and washing feet to ankles.

Reference: Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6; Sahih Muslim #223

Ghusl

Ghusl
غُسْل

Complete full-body ritual purification washing every hair and surface of the skin with water, mandatory after marital relations, seminal emission, menstruation, or post-partum bleeding.

Reference: Surah An-Nisa 4:43; Sahih al-Bukhari #248

Tayammum

Tayammum
تَيَمُّم

Dry purification performed using clean wholesome earth or dust when pure water is unavailable, insufficient, or medically harmful to use, substituting for Wudu and Ghusl.

Reference: Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6; Sahih al-Bukhari #335

Niyyah

Niyyah
نِيَّة

Conscious intention of the heart to perform an act of worship purely for the sake of Allah. An indispensable prerequisite (Shart) for the legal validity of all religious rites.

Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari #1; Sahih Muslim #1907

Qibla

Qiblah
قِبْلَة

The precise geographical direction toward the Holy Ka'bah in Makkah, which all Muslims across the globe must face during obligatory and voluntary prayers.

Reference: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:144; Sahih al-Bukhari #399

Khushoo'

Khushū'
خُشُوع

Deep spiritual humility, serenity, awe, and focused mental attentiveness during prayer and supplication, recognized as the inward soul of Salah.

Reference: Surah Al-Mu'minun 23:1-2; Sunan an-Nasa'i #1210

Fardh

Farḍ
فَرْض

Strictly obligatory duty established by definitive, unambiguous scriptural proof (e.g. the five daily prayers). Performance earns divine reward, intentional neglect warrants punishment, and denying its obligation constitutes disbelief.

Reference: Bada'i' as-Sana'i' by Al-Kasani; Al-Majmu' by An-Nawawi

Wajib

Wājib
وَاجِب

Mandatory religious obligation. In the Hanafi school, it is distinguished from Fardh as being established by probable (Zanni) rather than definitive (Qat'i) textual proof, such as Witr and Eid prayers.

Reference: Radd al-Muhtar by Ibn Abidin; Al-Hidayah by Al-Marghinani

Sunnah Mu'akkadah

Sunnah Mu'akkadah
سُنَّة مُؤَكَّدَة

Emphasized Sunnah practice that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ performed continuously and rarely abandoned, such as the two Rak'ahs before Fajr and four before Dhuhr.

Reference: Sahih Muslim #728; Sahih al-Bukhari #1180

Mustahabb

Mustaḥabb
مُسْتَحَبّ

Recommended or virtuous action whose performance is rewarded by Allah, but whose omission incurs no sin or penalty (also termed Mandub or Nafilah).

Reference: Al-Mustasfa by Imam al-Ghazali; Usul al-Fiqh by Al-Amidi

Makruh

Makrūh
مَكْرُوه

Disliked or detested action. Divided in Hanafi jurisprudence into Makruh Tanzihi (slightly disliked without sin) and Makruh Tahrimi (severely disliked, approaching Haram).

Reference: Al-Bahr ar-Ra'iq; Hashiyat Ibn Abidin

Haram

Ḥarām
حَرَام

Strictly forbidden and prohibited by unambiguous divine scripture. Engaging in it earns sin and divine retribution, while refraining out of piety is divinely rewarded.

Reference: Surah Al-An'am 6:151; Sahih al-Bukhari #52

Halal

Ḥalāl
حَلَال

Permissible, lawful, and wholesome in accordance with divine law, encompassing food, earnings, transactions, and lifestyle.

Reference: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:168; Sahih al-Bukhari #52

Takbirat al-Ihram

Takbīrat al-Iḥrām
تَكْبِيرَة الإِحْرَام

The opening proclamation of 'Allahu Akbar' that officially commences the prayer, rendering all mundane worldly actions (speaking, eating, walking) temporarily prohibited.

Reference: Sunan Abi Dawud #618; Sahih Muslim #397

Ruku'

Rukū'
رُكُوع

The bowing posture in prayer where the worshiper bends horizontally with hands grasping the knees, glorifying Allah with 'Subhana Rabbiyal-Azeem'.

Reference: Surah Al-Hajj 22:77; Sahih al-Bukhari #792

Sujud

Sujūd
سُجُود

Muqatta'at

Muqaṭṭa'āt
مُقَطَّعَات

The disjointed, mystical Arabic letters appearing at the beginning of 29 Surahs of the Holy Quran (e.g. Alif-Lam-Mim, Ha-Mim, Ya-Sin), symbolizing the divine inimitable miracle of the Quran.

Reference: Tafsir Ibn Kathir 1/157; Al-Itqan by As-Suyuti

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Authoritative answers regarding our scriptural sources, calculation algorithms, privacy model, and offline usage

What Quranic text and narration standard does NoorDeen utilize? ▼

NoorDeen reads the Qur'an in the verified Tanzil Uthmani script adhering strictly to the canonical Hafs 'an 'Asim narration, with all 114 Surahs and 6,236 Ayahs available in the reader. The complete text is bundled locally — with English, Urdu and transliteration for every ayah — and pre-cached by the service worker for offline reading. The Arabic script conforms to the King Fahd Glorious Quran Printing Complex standard in Madinah al-Munawwarah. Every verse is displayed with diacritics (Tashkeel) and pauses, and translations can be toggled without mutating or altering the sacred Arabic archetype.

How are Hadith narrations selected, sourced, and authenticated? ▼

Every tradition in our Hadith library is sourced directly from the recognized canonical collections (Kutub al-Sittah): Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Sunan an-Nasa'i, and Sunan Ibn Majah. Each entry features full Arabic text with Tashkeel, the primary narrator chain, collection and book citation, Hadith numbering, and explicit scholarly grading (e.g., Muttafaqun 'Alayh, Sahih, or Hasan). We strictly exclude unauthenticated or fabricated (Mawdu') narrations.

How are astronomical prayer times and solar angles calculated? ▼

Prayer times are calculated entirely on your device using high-precision astronomical solar positioning algorithms. The engine determines the solar transit (zenith/Zawal), equation of time, and solar declination based on your coordinates. We support internationally recognized calculation conventions including the University of Islamic Sciences Karachi (18° Fajr / 18° Isha), Muslim World League (18° / 17°), Islamic Society of North America (15° / 15°), and Umm al-Qura Makkah. Users can also select between Hanafi (shadow length ratio 2) and Shafi'i/Maliki/Hanbali (shadow length ratio 1) for Asr prayer.

Why does the Zakat Calculator offer both Gold and Silver Nisab options? ▼

Classical Islamic jurisprudence established two standards for the poverty threshold (Nisab): 20 Dinars of Gold (85g metric / 87.48g Hanafi) and 200 Dirhams of Silver (595g metric / 612.36g Hanafi). In the modern global economy, the silver threshold represents a substantially lower monetary value, meaning more individuals become liable to pay Zakat, which directly maximizes support for the eight Quranic recipient categories (Masarif al-Zakat, Surah At-Tawbah 9:60). Conversely, some jurists advocate the gold standard to reflect original purchasing power. NoorDeen transparently provides both standards with configurable precious metal valuations.

Can NoorDeen be installed and accessed completely offline? ▼

Yes. NoorDeen is engineered as an offline-first Progressive Web App (PWA). Modern browsers (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Brave) will prompt you to install the portal to your home screen or desktop. Once cached by our Service Worker, all 25 pages, Quran scriptures, Hadith texts, and calculation engines operate seamlessly without an internet connection. Furthermore, a standalone 25-page offline desktop bundle (ZIP) is available from our Download Center with SHA-256 verification.

Are my prayer locations, calculation numbers, or reading bookmarks stored on remote servers? ▼

No. NoorDeen operates under a strict zero-telemetry, client-side privacy architecture. Your GPS coordinates, city selections, Zakat financial numbers, Quran reading bookmarks, and Tasbih counts remain 100% within your device's browser local storage (localStorage). We do not operate remote tracking servers, advertising cookies, or behavioral trackers.
