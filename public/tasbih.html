The Spiritual Science & Virtues of Dhikr (Remembrance of Allah)

Exploring the theological excellence of constant remembrance, authentic post-prayer formulas, and classical etiquette.

The Quranic Call to Constant Remembrance

Throughout the Holy Quran, believers are commanded not merely to remember Allah occasionally, but to engage in Dhikran Katheera (abundant, continuous remembrance): "O you who have believed, remember Allah with much remembrance and exalt Him morning and afternoon." (Surah Al-Ahzab 33:41-42).

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ illustrated the stark contrast between those who maintain the life of the spirit through Dhikr and those who neglect it: "The likeness of the one who remembers his Lord and the one who does not remember his Lord is like the living and the dead." (Sahih al-Bukhari #6407). While the physical body requires water, oxygen, and nutrients, the spiritual heart suffocates and withers into spiritual death when starved of the remembrance of its Maker.

The Post-Salah Adhkar (Tasbih Fatimi)

Abu Hurayrah (RA) reported that the Prophet ﷺ prescribed the quintessential post-prayer remembrance:

  • Subhanallah (سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ): 33 Times (Glory be to Allah).
  • Alhamdulillah (الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ): 33 Times (All praise is due to Allah).
  • Allahu Akbar (اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ): 33 Times (Allah is the Greatest).
  • Sealing the 100th Recitation:
لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ، وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

Lā ilāha illallāhu waḥdahū lā shareeka lah, lahul-mulku wa lahul-ḥamdu, wa huwa ‘alā kulli shay’in qadeer.

"Whoever says this after every prayer will have his sins forgiven even if they were like the foam of the sea." (Sahih Muslim #597).

Counting on Fingers versus Using a Tasbih / Digital Counter

Abdullah ibn Amr (RA) narrated: "I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ counting the glorifications on the fingers of his right hand." (Sunan Abi Dawud #1502). The Prophet ﷺ explained that bodily fingers are endowed with speech and will testify on the Day of Resurrection to their owner's devotion.

However, when the Prophet ﷺ observed his wife Juwayriyah and other companions using date stones or pebbles to count large volumes of tasbeeh, he did not forbid them; instead, he approved the practice while teaching them more concise formulas. Prominent scholars across all four schools of law (including Ibn Taymiyyah and Imam an-Nawawi) affirmed that utilizing physical prayer beads or digital electronic counters is completely permissible without dislike, particularly for individuals striving to achieve high targets (e.g. 1,000 Salawat or Istighfar daily) without losing count.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dhikr

Is it better to count on fingers or use a digital counter?

Counting on the fingers of the right hand is the preferred prophetic Sunnah, as the fingers will testify on the Day of Judgment. However, using digital counters or prayer beads is fully permissible and widely recommended by scholars to maintain concentration during high-volume remembrance.

What is the reward of saying Subhanallahi wa bihamdihi 100 times?

The Prophet ﷺ declared: 'Whoever says: Subhanallahi wa bihamdihi (Glory and praise be to Allah) 100 times a day will have his sins forgiven even if they were as abundant as the foam of the ocean' (Sahih al-Bukhari #6405).

What are the four most beloved words to Allah?

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said: 'The most beloved words to Allah are four: Subhanallah, Alhamdulillah, La ilaha illallah, and Allahu Akbar; it does not matter with which of them you begin' (Sahih Muslim #2137).
