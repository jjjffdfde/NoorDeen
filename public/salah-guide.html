1. The Spiritual Dimension and Wisdom (Hikmah) of Salah

In Islamic theology, Salah (prayer) is not merely a ritualistic physical exercise or an arbitrary mechanical obligation; it represents the direct, unmediated spiritual audience (Munajah) between the human servant and Allah, the Creator and Sustainer of the cosmos. Revealed directly to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ during the heavenly ascent of the Miraj without the intermediary of Angel Jibril, Salah serves as the central anchor of a believer's daily life, dividing the 24-hour cycle into deliberate pauses of divine contemplation, humility, and moral realignment.

The Holy Quran declares: "Indeed, prayer restrains from immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater." (Surah Al-Ankabut 29:45). By prostrating the highest and most noble part of the human anatomy—the forehead—flat upon the dust in complete submission, the ego is purified of pride, arrogance, and worldly illusions. In a renowned tradition documented in Sahih al-Bukhari #528, the Prophet ﷺ asked his companions: "If there was a river at the door of one of you in which he bathed five times a day, would any dirt remain on him?" They replied: "None of his dirt would remain." The Prophet ﷺ said: "That is the likeness of the five daily prayers by which Allah erases sins."

2. Prerequisites of Salah (Shurut al-Salah)

Before initiating the prayer, nine essential conditions (Shurut) must be fulfilled. If any of these conditions is lacking without a legitimate Shar'i excuse, the prayer is invalid:

  1. Islam: Salah is an act of worship binding upon and accepted only from a Muslim.
  2. Sanity ('Aql): The worshipper must possess mental capacity and sound discernment.
  3. Discernment (Tamyiz): Reaching the age of comprehension (typically around seven years of age).
  4. Ritual Purity from Minor Impurity: Performing Wudu (ablution) or Tayammum (dry ablution) if water is inaccessible or harmful.
  5. Ritual Purity from Major Impurity: Performing Ghusl (full ritual bath) following post-sexual defilement (Janabah), menses (Hayd), or post-natal bleeding (Nifas).
  6. Purification of Body, Clothes, and Ground (Taharat al-Khabath): Ensuring total freedom from physical impurities (such as urine, blood, or feces).
  7. Covering the Awrah (Satr al-Awrah): For adult men, from the navel to below the knees, with shoulders covered. For adult women, the entire body except the face and hands (some jurists include feet).
  8. Commencement of the Prescribed Time (Dukhul al-Waqt): A prayer offered before its valid time is void.
  9. Facing the Qiblah (Istiqbal al-Qiblah): Aligning the body toward the Ka'bah in Makkah, Saudi Arabia.
  10. Intention (Niyyah): Internal determination in the heart designating the specific prayer being offered (verbalization is optional or disputed, but inward certainty is mandatory).

3. Daily 5 Prayers Rak'ah Breakdown Table

The five daily prayers consist of obligatory units (Fard), emphasized prophetic practices (Sunnah Mu'akkadah), non-emphasized prophetic prayers (Sunnah Ghayr Mu'akkadah), and voluntary night supplications (Witr & Nafl).

Fajr (Dawn) 2 Sunnah Mu'akkadah 2 Fard (Loud) — — 4 Rak'ahs

4. Step-by-Step Postures & Authentic Recitations

Follow each posture with physical stillness and deliberate contemplation. Every recitation is provided with complete Arabic diacritics, Romanized transliteration, English translation, and Urdu rendering.

POSTURE 1 تكبيرة الإحرام

Takbir al-Ihram (The Opening Takbir)

Stand upright with your feet naturally separated (shoulder-width), your gaze lowered toward the spot of prostration. Raise both hands with palms facing forward toward the Qiblah, fingers naturally open, aligned either with your shoulders or earlobes (both are authentic Sunnah practices). Utter with your tongue:

اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

Allāhu Akbar

English Meaning: Allah is the Greatest.

اللہ سب سے بڑا ہے۔

Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari #735, Sahih Muslim #390.
POSTURE 2 القيام ودعاء الاستفتاح

Qiyam (Standing) & Opening Supplication (Thana / Du'a al-Istiftah)

Fold your hands by placing the palm of your right hand over the back of your left wrist and forearm, resting upon the chest (Shafi'i & Hanbali practice) or below the navel (Hanafi practice). Recite the opening glorification silently:

سُبْحَانَكَ اللَّهُمَّ وَبِحَمْدِكَ، وَتَبَارَكَ اسْمُكَ، وَتَعَالَىٰ جَدُّكَ، وَلَا إِلَٰهَ غَيْرُكَ.

Subḥānakallāhumma wa bi-ḥamdika, wa tabārakas-muka, wa ta‘ālā jadduka, wa lā ilāha ghayruk.

English Meaning: Glory be to You, O Allah, and all praise is Yours; blessed is Your name, exalted is Your majesty, and there is no deity worthy of worship besides You.

پاک ہے تیری ذات اے اللہ اور تیرے ہی لیے حمد ہے، اور با برکت ہے تیرا نام اور بلند و بالا ہے تیری شان، اور تیرے سوا کوئی معبود نہیں۔

Reference: Sunan Abi Dawud #776, Jami` at-Tirmidhi #243, Sahih.
POSTURE 3 قراءة الفاتحة وسورة

Recitation of Surah Al-Fatihah and an Additional Surah

Seek refuge from Shaytan and begin in the name of Allah: "A'ūdhu billāhi minash-shayṭānir-rajīm. Bismillāhir-Raḥmānir-Raḥīm." Then recite Surah Al-Fatihah, which is an essential pillar of every Rak'ah:

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ ۝ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ۝ مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ ۝ إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ ۝ اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ ۝ صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ.

Conclude with "Āmīn" (آمين — "O Allah, answer our prayer"). In the first two Rak'ahs of any prayer, follow Al-Fatihah with at least one short Surah (such as Surah Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, or An-Nas) or at least three short verses from the Holy Quran.

"There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book (Surah Al-Fatihah)." (Sahih al-Bukhari #756, Sahih Muslim #394).
POSTURE 4 الركوع

Ruku' (The Bowing Posture)

Say "Allahu Akbar" (raising hands to shoulders/ears if following Raf' al-Yadayn) and bow at the waist. Keep your back completely horizontal and straight so that if water were poured upon it, it would settle flat. Grasp your knees firmly with your fingers spread like claws, elbows away from your sides, head neither dropped nor raised. Recite with tranquil devotion at least three times:

سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْعَظِيمِ

Subḥāna Rabbiyal-‘Aẓīm (3x)

English Meaning: Glory be to my Lord, the Supreme.

پاک ہے میرا پروردگار جو بڑی عظمت والا ہے۔

Reference: Sahih Muslim #772, Sunan Abi Dawud #870.
POSTURE 5 الرفع من الركوع والاعتدال

Qawmah (Standing Upright from Bowing)

Rise from the bowing posture until your spine is perfectly erect. As you straighten up, utter:

سَمِعَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ

Sami‘allāhu liman ḥamidah

"Allah hears the one who praises Him." (اللہ نے سن لیا اس شخص کی بات جس نے اس کی تعریف کی)

Once standing completely still and tranquil, recite:

رَبَّنَا وَلَكَ الْحَمْدُ، حَمْدًا كَثِيرًا طَيِّبًا مُبَارَكًا فِيهِ

Rabbanā wa lakal-ḥamd, ḥamdan katheeran ṭayyiban mubārakan feeh.

English Meaning: Our Lord, to You belongs all praise, abundant, pure, and full of blessings.

اے ہمارے رب! تیرے ہی لیے تمام حمد ہے، بہت زیادہ، پاکیزہ اور برکت والی تعریف۔

Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari #799. Over thirty angels competed to record this praise!
POSTURE 6 السجود على سبعة أعظم

Sujud (Prostration upon Seven Limbs)

Say "Allahu Akbar" and descend into prostration. The Prophet ﷺ commanded: "I was commanded to prostrate on seven bones: the forehead (pointing also to the nose), both hands, both knees, and the toes of both feet." (Sahih al-Bukhari #812). Keep your palms flat on the ground aligned with your shoulders/ears, fingers together pointing toward the Qiblah, forearms elevated off the ground (not resting like a dog), and toes flexed toward the Qiblah. Recite at least three times:

سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْأَعْلَىٰ

Subḥāna Rabbiyal-A‘lā (3x)

English Meaning: Glory be to my Lord, the Most High.

پاک ہے میرا رب جو سب سے بلند و بالا ہے۔

Reference: Sahih Muslim #482. The servant is closest to his Lord during Sujud.
POSTURE 7 الجلسة بين السجدتين

Jalsah (Sitting Between the Two Prostrations)

Say "Allahu Akbar" and rise to a calm, upright sitting posture. Sit upon your left foot laid flat (Iftirash) while keeping your right foot upright with toes pointing toward the Qiblah. Rest both hands upon your thighs near the knees and recite:

رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي، رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي، وَارْحَمْنِي، وَاهْدِنِي، وَارْزُقْنِي، وَعَافِنِي.

Rabbigh-fir lee, Rabbigh-fir lee, war-ḥamnee, wah-dinee, war-zuqnee, wa ‘āfinee.

English Meaning: My Lord, forgive me! My Lord, forgive me! Have mercy on me, guide me, provide for me, and grant me health and well-being.

اے میرے رب مجھے بخش دے، اے میرے رب مجھے بخش دے، مجھ پر رحم فرما، مجھے ہدایت دے، مجھے رزق عطا فرما، اور مجھے عافیت عطا فرما۔

Reference: Sunan Abi Dawud #874, Sunan Ibn Majah #897, Sahih.
POSTURE 8 السجدة الثانية والنهوض

Second Sujud & Completion of One Rak'ah

Say "Allahu Akbar" and perform the second prostration identically to the first, repeating "Subḥāna Rabbiyal-A‘lā" three times with complete tranquility. Once completed, one full Rak'ah is finished! Say "Allahu Akbar" and rise upright for the second Rak'ah (omitting the opening Thana, which is recited in the 1st Rak'ah only).

POSTURE 9 التشهد والتحيات

Tashahhud (The Sitting for Testimony)

Sit upright after the 2nd Rak'ah (and after the final Rak'ah). Form a loose circle with the thumb and middle finger of your right hand, or clench fingers gently while raising the right index finger pointing forward when pronouncing the Shahadah. Recite:

التَّحِيَّاتُ لِلَّهِ وَالصَّلَوَاتُ وَالطَّيِّبَاتُ، السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ، السَّلَامُ عَلَيْنَا وَعَلَىٰ عِبَادِ اللَّهِ الصَّالِحِينَ، أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ.

At-taḥiyyātu lillāhi waṣ-ṣalawātu waṭ-ṭayyibāt, as-salāmu ‘alayka ayyuhan-Nabiyyu wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh, as-salāmu ‘alaynā wa ‘alā ‘ibādillāhiṣ-ṣāliḥeen. Ash-hadu allā ilāha illallāhu, wa ash-hadu anna Muḥammadan ‘abduhū wa Rasooluh.

English Meaning: All verbal greetings, prayers, and pure offerings belong exclusively to Allah. Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings. Peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of Allah. I bear witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger.

تمام قولی، بدنی اور مالی عبادتیں اللہ ہی کے لیے ہیں۔ سلام ہو آپ پر اے نبی اور اللہ کی رحمت اور اس کی برکتیں۔ سلامتی ہو ہم پر اور اللہ کے تمام نیک بندوں پر۔ میں گواہی دیتا ہوں کہ اللہ کے سوا کوئی معبود نہیں، اور میں گواہی دیتا ہوں کہ حضرت محمد ﷺ اس کے بندے اور رسول ہیں۔

Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari #831, Sahih Muslim #402 (Ibn Mas'ud narration).
POSTURE 10 الصلاة الإبراهيمية

Durood Ibrahim (Blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ)

In the final sitting (Qa'dah Akhirah), follow the Tashahhud immediately with Durood Ibrahim:

اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَىٰ مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ مُحَمَّدٍ، كَمَا صَلَّيْتَ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، إِنَّكَ حَمِيدٌ مَجِيدٌ. اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ عَلَىٰ مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ مُحَمَّدٍ، كَمَا بَارَكْتَ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، إِنَّكَ حَمِيدٌ مَجِيدٌ.

Allāhumma ṣalli ‘alā Muḥammadin wa ‘alā āli Muḥammad, kamā ṣallayta ‘alā Ibrāheema wa ‘alā āli Ibrāheem, innaka Ḥameedum Majeed. Allāhumma bārik ‘alā Muḥammadin wa ‘alā āli Muḥammad, kamā bārakta ‘alā Ibrāheema wa ‘alā āli Ibrāheem, innaka Ḥameedum Majeed.

English Meaning: O Allah, bestow peace upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, as You bestowed peace upon Ibrahim and upon the family of Ibrahim; indeed, You are Praiseworthy, Full of Glory. O Allah, bestow blessings upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, as You bestowed blessings upon Ibrahim and upon the family of Ibrahim; indeed, You are Praiseworthy, Full of Glory.

اے اللہ! رحمت نازل فرما حضرت محمد ﷺ پر اور ان کی آل پر جس طرح تو نے رحمت فرمائی حضرت ابراہیم علیہ السلام پر اور ان کی آل پر، بے شک تو تعریف کیا گیا اور بڑی بزرگی والا ہے۔ اے اللہ! برکت نازل فرما حضرت محمد ﷺ پر اور ان کی آل پر جس طرح تو نے برکت نازل فرمائی حضرت ابراہیم علیہ السلام پر اور ان کی آل پر، بے شک تو تعریف کیا گیا اور بڑی بزرگی والا ہے۔

Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari #3370, Sahih Muslim #405.
POSTURE 11 الدعاء قبل السلام

Supplication for Protection Before Tasleem

The Prophet ﷺ emphasized seeking refuge from four severe trials before ending the prayer (Sahih Muslim #588):

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ عَذَابِ جَهَنَّمَ، وَمِنْ عَذَابِ الْقَبْرِ، وَمِنْ فِتْنَةِ الْمَحْيَا وَالْمَمَاتِ، وَمِنْ شَرِّ فِتْنَةِ الْمَسِيحِ الدَّجَّالِ.

Allāhumma innee a‘ūdhu bika min ‘adhābi Jahannam, wa min ‘adhābil-qabr, wa min fitnatil-maḥyā wal-mamāt, wa min sharri fitnatil-Maseeḥid-Dajjāl.

English Meaning: O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the torment of Hellfire, from the punishment of the grave, from the trials of living and dying, and from the evil tribulations of the False Messiah (Dajjal).

اے اللہ! میں تیری پناہ مانگتا ہوں جہنم کے عذاب سے، اور قبر کے عذاب سے، اور زندگی اور موت کے فتنوں سے، اور کانے دجال کے فتنے کے شر سے۔

POSTURE 12 التسليم للخروج من الصلاة

Tasleem (The Concluding Salutation of Peace)

Conclude the prayer by turning your face to the right until your right cheek is visible from behind, uttering:

السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ

As-salāmu ‘alaykum wa raḥmatullāh

Then turn your face to the left until your left cheek is visible, repeating:

السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ

As-salāmu ‘alaykum wa raḥmatullāh

"May the peace and mercy of Allah be upon you." (آپ پر اللہ کی سلامتی اور اس کی رحمت ہو)

Reference: Sahih Muslim #582. With this, your prayer is gracefully completed!

5. Pillars (Arkan) versus Obligatory Acts (Wajibaat)

Islamic jurisprudence draws a critical distinction between the fundamental Pillars (Arkan) of Salah and its Obligatory Acts (Wajibaat). Understanding this distinction is vital for knowing how to rectify mistakes made during prayer:

The 14 Essential Pillars (Arkan)

A Pillar cannot be dropped intentionally or accidentally. If forgotten, it must be performed physically; Sujud al-Sahwi alone does not replace it.

  • Standing during obligatory prayers (for the capable).
  • Opening Takbir (Takbirat al-Ihram).
  • Recitation of Surah Al-Fatihah.
  • Bowing (Ruku').
  • Straightening up from bowing.
  • Standing upright with stillness (I'tidal).
  • Prostrating on seven limbs (Sujud).
  • Rising from prostration.
  • Sitting between the two prostrations.
  • Calmness and tranquility (Tuma'neenah) in every posture.
  • The final sitting (Qa'dah Akhirah).
  • The final Tashahhud.
  • Salawat upon the Prophet ﷺ.
  • The Tasleem and maintaining the prescribed order (Tarteeb).

Obligatory Acts (Wajibaat)

If intentionally omitted, prayer is invalid. If forgotten accidentally, it is rectified completely by performing Sujud al-Sahwi at the end.

  • All transitional Takbirs other than the opening Takbir.
  • Saying "Sami'allahu liman hamidah" when rising from Ruku'.
  • Saying "Rabbana wa lakal-hamd" while upright.
  • Saying "Subhana Rabbiyal-'Azeem" at least once in Ruku'.
  • Saying "Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la" at least once in Sujud.
  • Saying "Rabbigh-fir lee" between the two prostrations.
  • The first sitting (after 2nd Rak'ah in 3- or 4-Rak'ah prayers).
  • Reciting the first Tashahhud during the middle sitting.

6. Special Scenarios: Masbuq, Qasr & Praying on a Chair

How a Latecomer (Masbuq) Catches Up

A latecomer who arrives after the Imam has commenced prayer must pronounce Takbir al-Ihram while standing, then immediately move into whatever position the Imam occupies (whether bowing, prostrating, or sitting).

  • Catching a Rak'ah: If you join the Imam while he is in Ruku' and achieve tranquility in bowing before the Imam begins rising, that Rak'ah counts as caught. If the Imam began rising before you bowed, you missed that Rak'ah.
  • Completing Missed Rak'ahs: When the Imam recites the final Tasleem, do not say Salam. Stand up saying "Allahu Akbar" and make up your missed Rak'ahs in their proper order.

Shortening & Combining Prayers During Travel (Qasr & Jam')

Islam grants travelers a gracious divine dispensation (Rukhsah) to shorten four-Rak'ah prayers to two Rak'ahs:

  • Which Prayers are Shortened: Dhuhr (from 4 to 2), Asr (from 4 to 2), and Isha (from 4 to 2). Fajr (2) and Maghrib (3) are never shortened.
  • Travel Distance: Approximately 48 miles (77–80 km) or more away from one's city boundaries.
  • Combining (Jam'): Permissible to combine Dhuhr with Asr (either at Dhuhr time [Taqdeem] or Asr time [Ta'kheer]), and Maghrib with Isha during active journeying, severe rain, or extreme hardship.

Rules for Praying on a Chair (Sick, Elderly & Injured)

The Prophet ﷺ stated to Imran ibn Husayn: "Pray standing; if you are unable, then sitting; and if you are unable, then lying on your side." (Sahih al-Bukhari #1117).

  • Stand if Able: If you can stand without severe injury or dizziness, you must do Takbir al-Ihram and recite Al-Fatihah while standing. You may then sit on the chair for bowing and prostrating.
  • Bowing & Prostrating on a Chair: Bend your upper torso forward slightly for Ruku'. For Sujud, bend your torso noticeably lower than in Ruku'. Placing a pillow or board in front of you to touch your head is contrary to the Sunnah and unnecessary.
  • Chair Alignment in Congregational Rows: Align the rear legs of the chair with the row of worshipers so that your back does not disrupt the line behind you.

7. Sujud al-Sahwi (Prostrations of Forgetfulness)

Because human beings are inherently subject to distraction and forgetfulness, Allah prescribed two prostrations at the conclusion of prayer (Sujud al-Sahwi) to repair flaws and humiliate Shaytan:

  • Due to Addition (Ziyadah): If you accidentally performed an extra Rak'ah, extra bowing, or extra prostration, perform two prostrations after reciting the final Tasleem, followed by another Tasleem.
  • Due to Omission (Naqs): If you omitted an obligatory act (such as forgetting the middle Tashahhud and rising straight to the 3rd Rak'ah), perform two prostrations before the Tasleem, then say Salam.
  • Due to Doubt (Shakk): If unsure whether you prayed 3 or 4 Rak'ahs:
    • Discard the doubt and build upon certainty (the lesser number, i.e. 3).
    • Complete the 4th Rak'ah, then perform two prostrations before saying Salam (Sahih Muslim #571).

8. Common Mistakes to Avoid (Aam Ghalatiyan)

Scholars and hadith commentators have identified several frequent mistakes that diminish the reward of prayer or invalidate it entirely:

❌ 1. Rushing Without Tranquility (Lack of Tuma'neenah)

Pecking at the ground like a crow without allowing every vertebrate to settle in Ruku', Qawmah, and Sujud voids the prayer. The Prophet ﷺ told a man who prayed quickly three times: "Go back and pray, for you have not prayed!" (Sahih al-Bukhari #757).

❌ 2. Preceding or Rushing Before the Imam (Musabaqah)

Bowing or prostrating before the Imam is strictly prohibited. The Prophet ﷺ warned: "Does not the one who raises his head before the Imam fear that Allah may turn his head into the head of a donkey?" (Sahih al-Bukhari #691).

❌ 3. Resting Forearms Flat on the Floor in Sujud

Resting elbows and forearms along the carpet resembles a resting dog or predator. The Prophet ﷺ explicitly forbade this posture: "Be moderate in prostration, and let none of you spread his arms like a dog spreads them." (Sahih al-Bukhari #822).

❌ 4. Looking Up at the Ceiling or Wandering Gaze

A worshipper's gaze should remain anchored upon the place of prostration during standing, bowing, and prostration. The Prophet ﷺ cautioned strongly against gazing skyward during Salah (Sahih Muslim #428).

❌ 5. Exposing the Lower Back (Awrah) During Bowing

Wearing short t-shirts or low-waisted trousers often results in the lower back or upper buttocks becoming exposed when bowing or prostrating. If the Awrah is uncovered during Salah, the prayer becomes void according to all schools of jurisprudence.

9. Frequently Asked Questions (Fatawa & Clarifications)

Authoritative answers based upon classical Islamic jurisprudence (Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali) to help worshippers worship with clarity and certainty.

What actions nullify (break) the Salah?

Salah is invalidated by: (1) Anything that invalidates Wudu (passing wind, urine, etc.); (2) Speaking intentional words that are not part of Quranic or prophetic recitations; (3) Laughing audibly (which in the Hanafi school also invalidates Wudu if done during prayer); (4) Eating or drinking intentionally; (5) Excessive movements unrelated to prayer; (6) Turning the chest completely away from the Qiblah; (7) Exposing the Awrah; and (8) Omitting an essential pillar (Rukn) without rectifying it.

What should I do if I doubt whether I prayed 3 or 4 Rak'ahs?

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ instructed that when doubt arises, the worshipper should discard doubt and build upon what is certain (which is the lesser number, such as assuming 3 Rak'ahs), complete the remaining prayer, and perform two prostrations of forgetfulness (Sujud al-Sahwi) before or after the final Tasleem (Sahih Muslim #571).

Is it permissible to pray while sitting on a chair?

Yes. If you have severe illness, surgery, knee pain, or dizziness that prevents standing or making full prostration, you may pray sitting on a chair. If you can stand comfortably, perform the opening Takbir and recitation standing, then sit for Ruku' and Sujud. Make your Sujud motion noticeably lower than your Ruku' motion.

Can I pray Salah while looking at a book or smartphone screen?

In voluntary night prayers (Tahajjud / Taraweeh), reading from a Mushaf is permissible according to the Shafi'i, Hanbali, and Maliki schools based on the practice of Aisha (RA). In obligatory (Fard) prayers, it is strongly disliked (Makruh) or invalid in the Hanafi school due to excessive hand movement and looking away from the place of prostration.

How does a latecomer (Masbuq) complete missed Rak'ahs?

A latecomer joins the congregation immediately in whatever posture the Imam is in. When the Imam delivers the final Tasleem to exit the prayer, the latecomer does NOT say Salam; instead, they stand up saying "Allahu Akbar" and make up the missed Rak'ahs in order.

What is the ruling on shortening prayers (Qasr) during travel?

Shortening four-Rak'ah prayers (Dhuhr, Asr, and Isha) to two Rak'ahs is an emphatic Sunnah and divine gift for travelers traveling beyond approximately 48 miles (77–80 km). Fajr and Maghrib are never shortened.

Is Witr prayer obligatory (Fard), Wajib, or Sunnah?

In the Hanafi school, Witr is classified as Wajib (necessary obligation just below Fard). In the Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali schools, Witr is an emphatic Sunnah (Sunnah Mu'akkadah) of the highest order which the Prophet ﷺ never neglected even during travel.

What should I do if I sneeze or yawn during prayer?

If you sneeze, it is permissible to whisper "Al-hamdulillah" silently to yourself, but other worshipers should not say "Yarhamukallah" to you. If you need to yawn, try to suppress it as much as possible; if unable, cover your mouth with the back of your left hand.

How do I make up missed (Qada) prayers?

Missed prayers remain a binding spiritual debt owed to Allah and must be prayed as soon as remembered. The majority of jurists hold that missed prayers should be performed in chronological order by offering the Fard Rak'ahs of the missed prayer.

Does having an impurity on clothes invalidate the prayer?

Yes. Purity from gross physical impurities (Taharat al-Khabath) on body, clothing, and the spot of prostration is an essential condition. If an impurity greater than the size of a coin is present and known to the worshiper, the clothing must be washed or changed before praying.
