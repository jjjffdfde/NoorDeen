The Comprehensive Jurisprudence (Fiqh) of Zakat

A detailed scholarly guide covering Nisab benchmarks, retirement assets, real estate, eligible beneficiaries, and classical calculations.

The Gold vs Silver Nisab Benchmark Debate

The Nisab is the minimum qualifying quantum of surplus wealth a Muslim must own before Zakat becomes mandatory. In the era of the Prophet ﷺ, the monetary values of gold and silver were closely aligned in purchasing power at approximately a 1:10 ratio:

  • Gold Nisab: 20 Mithqals (Dinars) = 85 grams (approx. 2.73 troy ounces) of pure 24-karat gold.
  • Silver Nisab: 200 Dirhams = 595 grams (approx. 19.13 troy ounces) of pure silver.

In contemporary times, the global financial divergence between gold and silver has widened dramatically. The silver threshold represents a substantially lower monetary barrier (often between \$500–\$700) compared to the gold threshold (frequently exceeding \$5,500–\$6,500). Classical Hanafi jurists strongly recommend adopting the Silver Nisab for cash and liquid wealth because it maximizes aid to the poor (Anfa' lil-Fuqara). However, if one holds purely physical gold jewelry or bullion, the 85-gram gold threshold is applied.

Modern Zakatable Assets: Stocks, 401(k), Real Estate & Crypto

Stocks & Equities

• Active Day Trading: Zakat is paid on the full market value of the shares at 2.5%.
• Passive Long-Term: Zakat is paid only on the company's underlying zakatable current assets (cash + inventory + receivables minus short liabilities), which scholars estimate at 25% to 30% of the stock's market value.

Retirement Funds (401k & IRA)

If the account holder possesses legal access to withdraw funds before retirement age (subject to early penalties and taxes), Zakat is due annually on the net accessible cash balance after deducting mandatory taxes and early withdrawal penalties.

Real Estate & Property

Personal residential homes are exempt from Zakat. For rental properties, Zakat is not due on the capital building value, but rather on the net accumulated rental income remaining after maintenance expenses at 2.5%.

Cryptocurrency & Digital Assets

Cryptocurrencies held as trade merchandise or stores of wealth are subject to Zakat at 2.5% of their fair market value at the conclusion of the lunar Hawl, provided the owner remains above Nisab.

The Eight Eligible Recipient Categories (Masarif az-Zakat)

Allah the Almighty uniquely reserved the distribution categories of Zakat, leaving no room for human alteration, directly revealed in Surah At-Tawbah (9:60):

  1. Al-Fuqara (The Destitute Poor): Individuals who possess zero wealth or less than half of their basic necessities.
  2. Al-Masakeen (The Needy): Individuals with employment or modest income that falls short of meeting fundamental living costs.
  3. Al-'Amilina 'Alayha (Zakat Administrators): Appointed collectors and managers of official Zakat funds.
  4. Al-Mu'allafati Qulubuhum (Reconciling Hearts): New converts or vulnerable individuals whose faith is supported.
  5. Fir-Riqab (Freeing Captives): Emancipating enslaved persons, human trafficking victims, and wrongful prisoners.
  6. Al-Gharimin (Insolvent Debtors): Those burdened by crippling debts incurred for permissible or essential needs.
  7. Fi Sabilillah (In the Cause of Allah): Funding defensive warfare, Islamic research, Da'wah, and educational outreach.
  8. Ibn As-Sabil (The Stranded Traveler): Legitimate travelers cut off from funds far from their homeland.

Frequently Asked Questions About Zakat

Should I use the Gold Nisab or the Silver Nisab threshold?

Classical scholars recommend using the Silver Nisab (595 grams / 200 Dirhams) for cash, liquid savings, and trade merchandise because its lower financial threshold benefits the poor by bringing more wealth into communal circulation. The Gold Nisab (85 grams / 20 Dinars) is strictly applied if one's wealth is held predominantly in physical gold.

How is Zakat calculated on 401(k), IRA, or pension retirement accounts?

For accessible retirement funds where premature withdrawal is possible, contemporary scholars recommend paying Zakat on the net balance after deducting early-withdrawal penalties and applicable taxes, or paying 2.5% on the underlying zakatable portion (cash and tradeable equities) of the portfolio.

How is Zakat calculated on stocks and shares?

If shares are bought for active day trading, Zakat is paid on their total current market value at 2.5%. If shares are purchased for long-term passive dividend investment, Zakat is paid only on the underlying zakatable liquid assets of the corporation (typically estimated at 25–30% of market value).

Can Zakat be given to one's parents, children, or spouse?

No. Zakat cannot be given to one's ascendants (parents, grandparents) or descendants (children, grandchildren), nor to one's spouse, because a Muslim is already legally obligated to financially support them. However, giving Zakat to needy siblings, uncles, aunts, cousins, or in-laws is permitted and doubly rewarded.

Can long-term debts like home mortgages be deducted from Zakat?

The entire 30-year balance of a home mortgage cannot be deducted from liquid wealth, as this would exempt nearly all homeowners from paying Zakat. Jurists agree that only the immediate installment due in the current lunar month or upcoming year may be deducted as an active liability.
