The Noble Sahaba (رضي الله عنهم)

The Rightly Guided Caliphs and Companions who preserved, lived, and conveyed the message of Islam to the world.

Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (رضي الله عنه)

First Caliph of Islam (11–13 AH) • Companion in the Cave
أبو بكر الصديق

The first adult male to accept Islam without hesitation. He spent his entire wealth freeing slaves, accompanied the Prophet ﷺ in the Cave of Thawr during the Hijrah, unified the Ummah after the Prophet's passing, and commissioned the compilation of the Holy Quran into a single Mushaf.

"Whoever worshipped Muhammad, know that Muhammad has passed away; but whoever worships Allah, know that Allah is Ever-Living and does not die."

Umar ibn al-Khattab (رضي الله عنه)

Second Caliph (13–23 AH) • Al-Faruq (The Criterion)
عمر بن الخطاب

His conversion granted the early Muslims strength to pray openly at the Ka'bah. Renowned for legendary justice and egalitarian governance. He established the welfare treasury (Bayt al-Mal), postal routes, the Hijri calendar, and granted the historic Treaty of Jerusalem protecting Christian places of worship.

"Since when did you enslave the people, when their mothers gave birth to them as free human beings?"

Uthman ibn Affan (رضي الله عنه)

Third Caliph (23–35 AH) • Dhun-Nurayn
عثمان بن عفان

Renowned for extraordinary modesty and generosity. Financed the expansion of Masjid an-Nabawi and purchased the Well of Rumah for public use. Standardized the official written copies of the Holy Quran, safeguarding unified recitation across all generations.

"If our hearts were truly pure, they would never tire of reciting the words of our Lord."

Ali ibn Abi Talib (رضي الله عنه)

Fourth Caliph (35–40 AH) • Gate to the City of Knowledge
علي بن أبي طالب

The first youth to embrace Islam. Cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet ﷺ, father of Hasan and Husayn. Famed for supreme bravery on the battlefield, profound legal knowledge, and unmatched mastery of Arabic eloquence and judicial justice.

"People are of two types: either your brothers in faith, or your equals in humanity."

Bilal ibn Rabah (رضي الله عنه)

Mu'adhin of the Messenger ﷺ • Emblem of Racial Equality
بلال بن رباح

Endured boiling desert rocks under torture in Makkah chanting "Ahad! Ahad!" (Allah is One!). Chosen by the Prophet ﷺ to call the first Adhan in Madinah and atop the Ka'bah during the conquest of Makkah, demonstrating Islam's total eradication of tribal and racial bias.
