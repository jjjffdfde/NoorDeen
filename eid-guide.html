Complete Step-by-Step Eid Prayer Guide

"Every nation has its festival, and this is our festival." (Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — Sahih al-Bukhari #952). Master the 6 Takbirs of Hanafi Fiqh vs 12 Takbirs of Shafi'i/Hanbali Fiqh, complete Takbirat al-Tashreeq, Zakat al-Fitr, Qurbani rules, and Sunan etiquettes.

1. Spiritual Wisdom (Hikmah) of the Two Islamic Eids

In Islam, celebration is inextricably linked to gratitude, devotion, and collective worship. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ arrived in Madinah, he found the people observing two days of pagan festivity. He said to them: "Allah has given you something better in their place: the Day of Eid al-Fitr and the Day of Eid al-Adha." (Sunan Abi Dawud #1134).

Eid al-Fitr (the Festival of Breaking the Fast) crowns the completion of the blessed month of Ramadan, expressing rejoicing over the divine guidance of the Quran and the strength given to fast and stand in night prayers. Eid al-Adha (the Festival of Sacrifice) culminates the monumental rites of Hajj, commemorating the heroic obedience of Prophet Ibrahim (AS) and his son Ismail (AS). Both celebrations begin not with frivolous amusement, but with a grand communal gathering in the open prayer grounds (Musalla) echoing with celestial Takbirs.

2. Timing, Prerequisites & Legal Ruling

  • Prescribed Timing: The time for Eid prayer begins approximately 15–20 minutes after sunrise (when the sun has risen above the horizon to the height of a spear, exiting the forbidden prayer time) and extends until just before the zenith (Zawal / solar noon).
  • No Adhan and No Iqamah: Jabir ibn Samurah narrated: "I prayed with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ the two Eid prayers more than once or twice, without an Adhan or an Iqamah." (Sahih Muslim #885).
  • The Khutbah is After the Prayer: Unlike the Friday Jumu'ah prayer where the sermon precedes the prayer, in Eid the two-Rak'ah prayer is offered first, followed by the Imam's sermon (Sahih al-Bukhari #956). Listening to the Khutbah is Sunnah and highly recommended.
  • Legal Status by Madhhab:
    • Hanafi: Wajib (obligatory) upon all men obligated to pray Jumu'ah.
    • Hanbali: Fard Kifayah (communal obligation).
    • Shafi'i & Maliki: Emphatic Sunnah Mu'akkadah for men, women, and children.

3. METHOD 1 (HANAFI MADHHAB): 6 EXTRA TAKBIRS Step-by-Step

In the Hanafi school (widely practiced across Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Turkey, Central Asia, and the Balkans), the prayer consists of 3 extra Takbirs in the first Rak'ah and 3 extra Takbirs in the second Rak'ah (total 6 extra Takbirs):

First Rak'ah (Hanafi Procedure)

  1. Make the intention (Niyyah) in the heart: "I intend to offer 2 Rak'ahs of Wajib Eid prayer with 6 extra Takbirs behind this Imam."
  2. Takbir al-Ihram: Raise hands to earlobes, say "Allahu Akbar", and fold hands below the navel.
  3. Recite the opening Thana silently: "Subḥānakallāhumma wa bi-ḥamdika..."
  4. 1st Extra Takbir: The Imam says "Allahu Akbar". Raise both hands to ears, then drop them to your sides (do not fold).
  5. 2nd Extra Takbir: The Imam says "Allahu Akbar". Raise hands to ears, then drop them to your sides.
  6. 3rd Extra Takbir: The Imam says "Allahu Akbar". Raise hands to ears, and now fold hands below the navel!
  7. The Imam recites Ta'awwudh, Tasmiyah, Surah Al-Fatihah, and an additional Surah loudly (such as Surah Al-A'la).
  8. Say "Allahu Akbar" and proceed to standard Ruku' and two Sujuds as usual.

Second Rak'ah (Hanafi Procedure)

  1. Rise from the second Sujud saying "Allahu Akbar" and fold hands immediately.
  2. The Imam recites Surah Al-Fatihah and an additional Surah loudly (such as Surah Al-Ghashiyah).
  3. After the recitation is complete, the extra Takbirs begin:
  4. 1st Extra Takbir: Say "Allahu Akbar", raise hands to ears, and release them to your sides.
  5. 2nd Extra Takbir: Say "Allahu Akbar", raise hands to ears, and release them to your sides.
  6. 3rd Extra Takbir: Say "Allahu Akbar", raise hands to ears, and release them to your sides.
  7. Ruku' Takbir (4th Takbir): Say "Allahu Akbar" without raising hands, and bow directly into Ruku'!
  8. Complete the two Sujuds, Tashahhud, Durood Ibrahim, and final Tasleem normally.

4. METHOD 2 (SHAFI'I & HANBALI): 12 EXTRA TAKBIRS Step-by-Step

In the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools (widespread across the Arab world, Southeast Asia, East Africa, and North America), the prayer features 7 extra Takbirs in the first Rak'ah and 5 extra Takbirs in the second Rak'ah:

First Rak'ah (Shafi'i & Hanbali Procedure)

  • Recite Takbir al-Ihram (saying "Allahu Akbar") and fold hands across the chest.
  • Recite the opening Du'a al-Istiftah.
  • The Imam then pronounces 7 consecutive Takbirs. With each Takbir, raise your hands to your shoulders/ears. Between each Takbir, say silently:
    "Subḥānallāh, wal-ḥamdu lillāh, wa lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu Akbar" (سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ).
  • Recite Ta'awwudh and Surah Al-Fatihah, followed by Surah Qaf or Surah Al-A'la.
  • Complete the Ruku' and two Sujuds as usual.

Second Rak'ah (Shafi'i & Hanbali Procedure)

  • Rise from Sujud saying the transitional Takbir (Takbir al-Qiyam).
  • The Imam then pronounces 5 consecutive Takbirs before reciting Al-Fatihah. Raise hands with each Takbir and glorify Allah between them.
  • Recite Surah Al-Fatihah and an additional Surah (such as Surah Al-Qamar or Surah Al-Ghashiyah).
  • Bow into Ruku', complete Sujuds, Tashahhud, and the Tasleem.

5. Takbirat al-Tashreeq: Complete Text, Translation & Schedule

During Eid al-Adha and the Days of Tashreeq, reciting the Takbirs loudly after every obligatory (Fard) prayer is an emphatic Sunnah and Wajib in many schools:

The Authentic Takbirat al-Tashreeq:
اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، وَلِلَّهِ الْحَمْدُ.

Allāhu Akbar, Allāhu Akbar, Lā ilāha illallāhu wallāhu Akbar, Allāhu Akbar, wa lillāhil-ḥamd.

English Meaning: Allah is the Greatest, Allah is the Greatest! There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah, and Allah is the Greatest, Allah is the Greatest, and to Allah belongs all praise!

اللہ سب سے بڑا ہے، اللہ سب سے بڑا ہے، اللہ کے سوا کوئی معبود نہیں، اور اللہ سب سے بڑا ہے، اللہ سب سے بڑا ہے، اور تمام تعریفیں اللہ ہی کے لیے ہیں۔

Prescribed Schedule (23 Consecutive Prayers):

  • Begins: After Fajr prayer on the Day of Arafah (9th of Dhul Hijjah).
  • Continues: After every Fard prayer throughout the 10th (Eid Day), 11th, and 12th of Dhul Hijjah.
  • Ends: After the Asr prayer on the 13th of Dhul Hijjah.
  • Total: Exactly 23 consecutive obligatory prayers! Men recite audibly; women recite softly.

6. Sunnah Etiquettes of the Day of Eid

  1. Ghusl (Ritual Bath): Taking a full bath before setting out for the prayer grounds (Muwatta Malik #428).
  2. Wearing One's Best Clothes & Perfume: Dressing in clean, fine garments and applying pleasant scent (for men).
  3. Eating Odd-Numbered Dates on Eid al-Fitr: The Prophet ﷺ never departed for Eid al-Fitr without first eating an odd number of dates (Sahih al-Bukhari #953). On Eid al-Adha, he delayed eating until after the prayer to eat from his sacrifice.
  4. Reciting Takbir En Route: Proclaiming Takbirs loudly while walking to the prayer ground.
  5. Walking to the Musalla & Taking Alternate Routes: Going to prayer by one path and returning home by a different path to greet more people and have more ground bear witness (Sahih al-Bukhari #986).
  6. Congratulating One Another: The Sahaba greeted each other saying: "Taqabbalallāhu minnā wa minkum" (تَقَبَّلَ اللَّهُ مِنَّا وَمِنْكُمْ — "May Allah accept from us and from you").

7. Zakat al-Fitr & Qurbani (Udhiyyah) Rules

Zakat al-Fitr (Fitrana)

Mandatory upon every Muslim who possesses food exceeding one day's sustenance for themselves and their dependents. Equivalent to 1 Sa' (approx. 2.5 to 3 kg) of staple food (wheat, barley, dates, rice) or its monetary equivalent. Must be paid before the Eid prayer so that the needy can rejoice on Eid.

Qurbani / Udhiyyah

The ritual sacrifice of livestock (sheep, goat, cow, camel) after the Eid al-Adha prayer. Valid across the 10th, 11th, 12th, and 13th of Dhul Hijjah. Meat is traditionally distributed in three portions: one for the family, one for relatives/friends, and one for the poor.

8. Edge Cases & Special Scenarios

Eid Coinciding with Friday (Jumu'ah)

When Eid falls on Friday, worshippers who attend the Eid prayer in the morning are granted a divine concession (Rukhsah) permitting them to pray 4 Rak'ahs of Dhuhr instead of attending the Friday Jumu'ah prayer (Sunan Abi Dawud #1070). However, the Imam is still obligated to hold Jumu'ah for those who wish to attend, and attending both is superior.

Making Up a Missed Eid Prayer

If you arrived at the Musalla after the prayer had concluded, you may pray two Rak'ahs at home with the extra Takbirs, as practiced by Anas ibn Malik (RA) when he missed Eid with the governor in Basra (Sahih al-Bukhari).

9. Common Mistakes to Avoid in Eid Prayer

❌ 1. Delaying Zakat al-Fitr Until After the Prayer

If given after the Eid prayer, it is recorded merely as ordinary charity (Sadaqah) rather than accepted Zakat al-Fitr (Sunan Abi Dawud #1609).

❌ 2. Praying Nafl (Voluntary) Prayers in the Musalla Before Eid

Ibn Abbas (RA) narrated: "The Prophet ﷺ went out on the Day of Eid and prayed two Rak'ahs, not praying anything before or after them." (Sahih al-Bukhari #989). If praying in an open Musalla, sit down immediately upon arrival and engage in Takbir.

❌ 3. Leaving the Mosque During the Sermon (Khutbah)

Rushing away the second the prayer finishes deprives one of the prophetic sermon, angelic blessings, and community supplications.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Eid prayer obligatory (Wajib, Fard, or Sunnah)?

In the Hanafi school, Eid prayer is Wajib (mandatory) upon all adult men who are required to attend Jumu'ah. In the Hanbali school, it is Fard Kifayah (communal obligation). In the Shafi'i and Maliki schools, it is an emphatic Sunnah Mu'akkadah that women and children are also strongly encouraged to attend.

What should I do if I miss the Eid prayer with the congregation?

According to the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools (and a narration from Anas ibn Malik in Sahih al-Bukhari), a person who missed the congregational Eid prayer may pray two Rak'ahs at home individually or with family, reciting the extra Takbirs. In the Hanafi school, there is no individual Qada for missed Eid prayer, but one may pray four Rak'ahs of voluntary Duha prayer instead.

What happens if Eid falls on a Friday (Jumu'ah)?

If a worshipper attends the Eid prayer in the morning, they are granted a dispensation (Rukhsah) permitting them to pray Dhuhr at home instead of attending Jumu'ah according to the Hanbali school based on the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ (Sunan Abi Dawud #1070). However, attending both Eid and Jumu'ah remains vastly more virtuous, and the Imam is obligated to establish Jumu'ah for those wishing to attend.

When do Takbirat al-Tashreeq begin and end?

Takbirat al-Tashreeq begin after the Fajr prayer on the Day of Arafah (9th of Dhul Hijjah) and continue after every obligatory (Fard) prayer until the Asr prayer on the 13th of Dhul Hijjah, totaling 23 consecutive prayers.

What is the difference between Hanafi 6 Takbirs and Shafi'i 12 Takbirs?

In the Hanafi school, there are 3 additional Takbirs in the 1st Rak'ah (after Thana and before Al-Fatihah) and 3 additional Takbirs in the 2nd Rak'ah (after recitation and before Ruku'). In the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools, there are 7 additional Takbirs in the 1st Rak'ah (after Takbir al-Ihram and before Al-Fatihah) and 5 additional Takbirs in the 2nd Rak'ah (after rising from Sujud and before Al-Fatihah).

Is there any Adhan or Iqamah for Eid prayer?

No. By unanimous scholarly consensus based on Sahih Muslim #885, there is neither an Adhan nor an Iqamah for Salat al-Eid. The prayer commences directly with the Imam's Takbir al-Ihram.

Can women and children attend Eid prayer?

Yes, it is an emphatic Sunnah for women, young girls, and children to attend the outdoor Eid prayer grounds (Musalla) to witness the blessings and supplications of the believers, as explicitly narrated by Umm Atiyyah in Sahih al-Bukhari #324.

When must Zakat al-Fitr be paid?

Zakat al-Fitr must be paid before the commencement of the Eid al-Fitr prayer on the morning of Eid. It may also be paid a day or two prior to Eid so that the poor can prepare for the festive day.
