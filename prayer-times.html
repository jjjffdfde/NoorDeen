The Science of Astronomical Solar Calculations in Islam

How classical Muslim astronomers harmonized celestial solar mechanics with prophetic juristic mandates.

Astronomical Criteria for the Five Daily Prayers

Unlike civil time which follows arbitrary artificial time zones, Islamic prayer times are strictly governed by the celestial mechanics of the sun relative to an observer's exact geographic coordinates:

Fajr (True Dawn — Subh Sadiq):

Astronomically defined when the center of the solar disc is between 18° and 19.5° below the eastern horizon (astronomical twilight). At this juncture, scattering of sunlight across the upper atmosphere produces a distinct horizontal band of white light spanning the horizon.

Sunrise (Shuruq):

The exact moment the upper limb of the sun touches the eastern horizon. End of Fajr prayer time; voluntary prayers become forbidden until the sun ascends.

Dhuhr (Solar Noon & Zawal):

Commences immediately after Zawal, the moment the sun passes the meridian (its highest point in the sky) and begins its downward trajectory toward the west.

Asr (Shadow Proportions):

• Mithl 1 (Majority: Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali): Begins when the shadow of an upright object equals its height plus noon shadow.
• Mithl 2 (Hanafi): Begins when the shadow equals twice the height of the object plus noon shadow.

Maghrib (Sunset — Ghurub):

Occurs the instant the entire solar disc vanishes below the western horizon. Fasting is broken immediately, and Maghrib prayer commences.

Isha (Disappearance of Twilight — Shafaq):

Occurs when the afterglow of sunset vanishes from the sky (sun 15° to 18° below the western horizon). In the Hanafi school (according to Imam Abu Hanifah), it is when the white twilight vanishes; according to the Two Companions (Abu Yusuf and Muhammad) and majority jurists, it is when the red twilight vanishes.

The Three Forbidden Times for Prayer (Auqat-e-Mamnoo'ah)

Uqbah ibn Amir (RA) reported: "There are three times during which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade us to pray or to bury our dead: (1) When the sun begins to rise until it is fully risen; (2) When the sun is at its height at midday until it passes the meridian; and (3) When the sun draws near to setting until it sets." (Sahih Muslim #831).

  • Sunrise: Approximately 15 to 20 minutes following the sunrise time displayed on schedules.
  • Midday Peak (Istiwa'): Approximately 5 to 10 minutes prior to the start of Dhuhr.
  • Sunset: When the sun turns reddish-orange and pale (approx. 15–20 minutes prior to Maghrib). However, if someone has not yet prayed Asr of that day, they must pray it immediately even during this time.

High-Latitude Calculations (Midnight Sun & Persistent Twilight)

In extreme northern and southern latitudes (e.g. Scandinavia, Scotland, Alaska, Canada, and Russia) during summer months, the sun never dips far enough below the horizon to produce astronomical darkness (the sun stays within 18° of the horizon all night).

The Islamic Fiqh Academy of the Muslim World League and the European Council for Fatwa and Research (ECFR) resolved that communities residing where true dawn or true twilight ceases must estimate their prayer times (Taqdeer). Accepted methods include using the Nearest Latitude (Aqrab al-Bilad) at 45° latitude, or applying the Middle of the Night (Nisf al-Layl) principle where the interval between Maghrib and sunrise is split evenly.

Frequently Asked Questions About Prayer Times

What is the difference between 18° and 15° Fajr calculation?

The 18-degree angle represents astronomical twilight (when the sun is 18 degrees below the horizon), adopted by the Muslim World League and Umm Al-Qura as true dawn (Subh Sadiq). The 15-degree angle represents marine twilight, used by ISNA in North America to prevent seasonal hardship during short summer nights.

Why do Hanafi and Standard Asr times differ?

In the Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali schools (Mithl 1), Asr begins when the shadow equals the object's height plus noon shadow. In the Hanafi school (Mithl 2), Asr begins when the shadow equals twice the object's height plus noon shadow, occurring about 45–60 minutes later.

What are the three forbidden times for voluntary prayers?

The three windows are: (1) During sunrise until 15–20 minutes after; (2) At exact solar noon (Istiwā') when the sun is at its apex; and (3) In the final 15–20 minutes before sunset when the sun turns pale yellow.

How are prayer times calculated in extreme high latitudes?

Where twilight persists all night, scholars mandate estimation (Taqdeer) using the Nearest Latitude method (borrowing times from 45° latitude), the Middle of the Night method, or the One-Seventh of the night rule.
