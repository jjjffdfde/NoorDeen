Stories of the Prophets (عليهم السلام)

"There was certainly in their stories a lesson for those of understanding." (Surah Yusuf 12:111). Explore the lives, unwavering monotheism, trials, and divine victories of the Prophets in Islam based on the Holy Quran and authentic Tafsir.

📜 Chronological Index: Prophetic Narratives in the Holy Quran

Qisas al-Anbiya

Grounded in the Holy Quran and classical exegesis (*Tafsir Ibn Kathir* and *Qisas al-Anbiya*), highlighting unwavering monotheism (Tawheed), supreme patience (Sabr), and moral fortitude.

Adam (عليه السلام)

Father of Mankind • Mentioned 25 Times in the Holy Quran
آدم عليه السلام

Created by Allah directly from earth and clay, honored when Allah commanded the angels to prostrate to him, and endowed with the divine names and knowledge of all things. When tempted by Iblis regarding the forbidden tree, Adam and Hawwa (Eve) turned in sincere, sorrowful repentance, teaching humanity the eternal door of Tawbah: "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers" (Surah Al-A'raf 7:23).

Eternal Theological Lessons: Supreme honor of divine knowledge • The destructive arrogance of Iblis (Kibr) • Sincere repentance instantly restores divine mercy.
Quranic References: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:30–39; Surah Al-A'raf 7:11–25; Surah Ta-Ha 20:115–123.

Nuh / Noah (عليه السلام)

Shaykh al-Anbiya • Preached Tawhid for 950 Years
نوح عليه السلام

Preached pure monotheism with unmatched perseverance day and night, openly and secretly, for nine and a half centuries. When the corrupt ruling oligarchs mocked him and persisted in idolatry, Allah commanded him to construct the Ark (Al-Fulk) under divine surveillance. The believers and animal pairs were delivered across the raging floodwaters while tyranny was washed away.

Eternal Theological Lessons: Tireless patience without conditioning efforts on immediate worldly numbers • True kinship is bound by faith, not biological bloodline alone.
Quranic References: Surah Hud 11:25–49; Surah Al-Mu'minun 23:23–30; Surah Nuh 71:1–28.

Ibrahim / Abraham (عليه السلام)

Khalilullah (Intimate Friend of Allah) • Builder of the Ka'bah
إبراهيم عليه السلام

The archetype of pure, uncompromised monotheism (Hanifiyyah). Cast into King Nimrod's blazing inferno for shattering idols, Allah commanded the flames: "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham!" (21:69). Tested with settling baby Ismail and Lady Hajar in the barren desert of Bakkah, and submitting to the divine command of sacrifice, he surrendered completely, earning Allah's praise as an entire nation (Ummah) within a single soul (Surah An-Nahl 16:120).

Eternal Theological Lessons: Absolute reliance upon Allah (Tawakkul) • Rational discourse against superstition • The foundational rites of Hajj and Qurbani.
Quranic References: Surah Al-Baqarah 2:124–132; Surah Al-An'am 6:74–83; Surah Ibrahim 14:35–41.

Yusuf / Joseph (عليه السلام)

As-Siddiq (The Truthful) • Surah Yusuf (The Best of Stories)
يوسف عليه السلام

Cast into a dark well by jealous brothers, sold into Egyptian enslavement, and falsely imprisoned for years when he chose prison over succumbing to the seductions of the governor's wife, declaring: "My Lord, prison is more desirable to me than that to which they invite me" (12:33). Through divine wisdom and dream interpretation, he was elevated to Minister of Treasury of Egypt, saving the region from catastrophic famine. When his brothers stood before him in humbled desperation, he displayed peerless forgiveness: "No blame will there be upon you today. Allah will forgive you; and He is the most merciful of the merciful" (Surah Yusuf 12:92).

Eternal Theological Lessons: Moral chastity and spiritual integrity under temptation • Complete trust in divine providence through trials • Sublime forgiveness without vengeance.
Quranic References: Entire Surah Yusuf (Surah 12:1–111); Ibn Kathir, Qisas al-Anbiya.

Ayyub / Job (عليه السلام)

Paragon of Sabr (Patience) • Tested in Health, Wealth & Family
أيوب عليه السلام

Endowed with vast wealth, flourishing livestock, and many sons, Ayyub was subjected to severe tribulation: losing all his wealth, his children, and suffering from debilitating physical affliction for years while remaining steadfast in dhikr and gratitude. His supplication was recorded as an eternal template of humble prayer: "Indeed, adversity has touched me, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful" (Surah Al-Anbiya 21:83). Allah commanded him to strike the earth with his foot, bringing forth a cool healing spring that restored his body, and restored his family and fortune doubled.

Eternal Theological Lessons: Sabr (patience) is not passive complaint, but active steadfastness • Trials do not indicate divine anger • Allah's relief surpasses all previous loss.
Quranic References: Surah Al-Anbiya 21:83–84; Surah Sad 38:41–44.

Yunus / Jonah (عليه السلام)

Dhun-Nun (Possessor of the Whale) • Prophet to Nineveh
يونس عليه السلام

Sent to the people of Nineveh in ancient Mesopotamia, he departed in frustration before receiving explicit divine command when they initially resisted. Boarding a storm-tossed vessel, lots were cast and he was cast into the sea, swallowed by a giant whale. From the triple darkness of the night, sea depths, and belly of the fish, he cried out in immortal repentance: "La ilaha illa Anta subhanaka inni kuntu minaz-zalimin" (There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers) (Surah Al-Anbiya 21:87). The Prophet ﷺ testified that no Muslim ever makes this supplication except that Allah relieves their anguish. Delivered onto shore, his entire nation of over 100,000 embraced the faith.

Eternal Theological Lessons: Sincere self-accountability before pointing fingers • The unmatched potency of Ayat al-Karimah in trials • Hope when all worldly exits vanish.
Quranic References: Surah Al-Anbiya 21:87–88; Surah As-Saffat 37:139–148; Surah Al-Qalam 68:48–50.

Musa / Moses (عليه السلام)

Kalimullah (Spoken to by Allah) • Liberator of the Children of Israel
موسى عليه السلام

Confronted the brutal totalitarian regime of Pharaoh with divine signs and the miraculous staff. When cornered against the roaring Red Sea with Pharaoh's chariots closing in behind, his companions cried: "Indeed, we are overtaken!" Musa answered with unshakeable certainty: "No! Indeed, with me is my Lord; He will guide me!" (Surah Ash-Shu'ara 26:61–62). Allah parted the sea into twelve paths of safety, drowned the oppressors, and revealed the Holy Torah at Mount Sinai.

Eternal Theological Lessons: Courage against tyranny in defense of the oppressed • Faith when all worldly doors appear locked • Divine law as a standard of justice.
Quranic References: Surah Al-Qasas 28:1–44; Surah Ta-Ha 20:9–98; Surah Ash-Shu'ara 26:10–68.

Dawud & Sulayman / David & Solomon (عليهما السلام)

Prophet-Kings • The Psalms (Az-Zabur) & Supreme Wisdom
داود وسليمان عليهما السلام

Dawud vanquished Jalut (Goliath) in youth, received the scripture of Az-Zabur, and was endowed with a voice so melodious that the mountains and birds echoed his hymns of praise (Tasbih). He ate only from the work of his own hands, softening iron into chainmail armour without fire. His son Sulayman inherited his wisdom and was granted dominion unmatched in human history: command over the winds, jinn, and the ability to understand the speech of birds and ants. When viewing his grand kingdom, Sulayman humbled himself: "This is from the favor of my Lord to test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful" (Surah An-Naml 27:40).

Eternal Theological Lessons: Wealth and power are tests of gratitude (Shukr) • Earning one's livelihood through manual skill • Judicial impartiality and wise arbitration.
Quranic References: Surah Al-Anbiya 21:78–82; Surah An-Naml 27:15–44; Surah Saba 34:10–14.

Isa / Jesus (عليه السلام)

Ruhullah & Kalimatullah • Son of Maryam (Virgin Mary)
عيسى عليه السلام

Born miraculously without a human father to the purest woman of all worlds, Sayyidah Maryam (RA). Spoke eloquently from the cradle defending his mother's purity, healed the blind and leper, and raised the dead by Allah's sovereign permission (bi-idhnillāh). The Quran clarifies that he was neither killed nor crucified, but raised bodily to heaven by Allah (Surah An-Nisa 4:157–158), and will descend before the Day of Judgment to establish justice and affirm the message of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

Eternal Theological Lessons: Rejection of both extreme slander and pagan deification • Ascetic detachment from worldly materialism • Unbroken lineage of Prophetic monotheism.
Quranic References: Surah Ali 'Imran 3:42–59; Surah Maryam 19:16–36; Surah An-Nisa 4:156–159.

Muhammad ﷺ — The Seal of the Prophets (خاتم النبيين)

Rahmatun lil-'Alamin (Mercy to the Worlds) • The Final Messenger
محمد رسول الله ﷺ

The culmination and seal of all divine prophecy: "Muhammad is not the father of any one of your men, but he is the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets" (Surah Al-Ahzab 33:40). Sent not to a single tribe or nation, but as a universal mercy to all mankind and jinn, bringing the final, incorruptible revelation of the Holy Quran.
